The time scale and navigation

On the map, the moments sit down a time scale at the right, roughly where their years fall, each with its title. A triangle beside it says where in time the map is: drag it, and the map follows; leave it between two moments, and inside a moment like 271–1000 the map shows what happened by then. The row of year buttons the map started with is gone from it (lesson 05 keeps it, with the shapes' working notes). Back and Next, swipes and keys for the whole page are still to come (section 6).

1. A time scale, roughly in proportion

  • Each moment sits where its year falls, from c. 60 BC at the top to 2007 at the bottom, so a child sees how long the Romans stayed against how quickly the 20th century went by. A strictly proportional scale crowds the last seven moments (1775 to 2007) into a tenth of its length, so moments closer than a minimum gap (5.5% of the length, room for a year and a title) are pushed apart, and the years in between are spread evenly from one moment to the next. The early centuries stay in proportion: c. 1000 is a little under halfway.
  • The line is the panel's own left edge, a strip down the whole right of the window, top to bottom. Each moment's dot sits on it, level with its years (larger, in the tracked capitals of the map's lettering), its title under them. A span's end is only written where it isn't the next moment's start: 101, not 101–271.
  • A black ear outside the line, under the header's links, folds the strip to the years only (its white caret points the way it goes) and opens it again; the browser remembers which. On a window narrower than 760 px the strip is hidden, so the caption can take the width (section 6).

2. The triangle

  • A triangle outside the line, pointing at it, is where in time the map is. It can rest anywhere: the map shows the moment before it. Only the triangle drags; a click on a moment's dot, years or title goes to that moment, and the triangle glides there. The arrow keys move it moment by moment once it has the keyboard (Home and End: the first and last), and a screen reader hears it as a slider reading out the years.
  • Dragging past moments with the same view (1859, 1878, 1918) changes the map as you go. A moment that would zoom (c. 60 BC, 2007) is only marked while you drag, its years in a dashed box, and shown when you let go: one zoom, not one for every moment passed. Each moment is asked for once, since a zoom out waits for its data (lesson 06).
  • The mouse wheel steps a moment at a time too: one gesture, one moment (a trackpad's coasting doesn't count as more), never sooner than 0.7 s after the last.

3. Inside a moment: 271–1000

  • The triangle's place is a year, not only a moment: halfway between 271 and c. 1000 is about 635. The map gets that year, and 271–1000's arrows follow it instead of looping: each people's arrow draws on over its own years (at least 60, so a short one like the Bulgars in 680 is still seen), stays 80 years after them, then fades over 60 to a faint trace, so the earlier invasions stay visible faintly. The big caption at the top names the people still on the move ("560: Avars"). Each arrow's years are numbers in data/moments.ts (years).
  • While 271 is chosen, the scale shows its events: a grey tick across the line for each people, with its first year and name (250 Goths, 375 Huns, 500 Slavs, 558 Avars, 670 Bulgars, 890 Magyars), each where its year falls but clear of 271's own label and of each other. A tick moves the triangle to its year. A small play button at the end of 271's row moves the triangle through to c. 1000 by itself, about 14 seconds, and stops just before it.
  • Chosen with a click, the wheel or the keys, the moment is shown whole: the arrows loop, one after another, as in lesson 06.

4. The panels

  • The caption, the moment's icon, title, years ago and story, is at the bottom left, with its key under it when the map has something it doesn't name itself. The page's title is there for screen readers only: the map says it. The backdrop tuner (development only, lesson 02) moved to the top left, out of the time scale's way.
  • Tried and dropped on the way: a second, horizontal scale along the bottom; a big play button for the whole story (too long to sit through: play now lives only where there is something to watch, 271–1000); the year buttons in a panel of their own.

5. Less motion

With reduced motion, the arrows show all at once, the triangle and the map change without gliding, and every zoom is instant.

6. Still to do

  • Back and Next. Big buttons, a child's size, by the caption: the one way through the story that needs no aim, and the one a phone has (the time scale is hidden on a narrow window, so today a phone can't change the moment at all).
  • Swipe. Left and right on the map for Next and Back, on a touch screen; the map doesn't pan or zoom by hand, so a swipe is free to mean that.
  • Keys for the whole page. ← and → (and Page Up / Page Down) for Back and Next without first giving the triangle the keyboard; Space for the small play on 271.
  • A narrator and the story read aloud were part of this lesson's first plan; they move to lesson 08, as an option.

Review checklist

  • The map: the time scale runs down the right edge, top to bottom; every moment's dot sits on the line, level with its years
  • Click 1600's title: 1600; the triangle glides to its dot
  • Drag the triangle from 1918 up to c. 60 BC: the map follows to c. 1000, then waits, marking the moment under the triangle, and zooms once on letting go
  • Choose 271: six grey ticks under it, and a small play; press it: the arrows draw in their own years, the caption names each people
  • The ear folds the strip to the years and opens it; a reload keeps it as it was
  • Scroll over the map: a moment per gesture
