The moments

The story's words: fourteen moments, from the Dacians to today, each a sentence or two a 7-to-10-year-old can read aloud. They live in one content file, data/moments.ts, next to each moment's map (lesson 05), and the map page tells them under the map. (Lesson 04 and 05 came first: the tool and the shapes, so the shapes could be built while the story was written.)

2. Thirteen, not eighteen

  • The first plan had eighteen moments. For a child, that's too many to hold: close events now share one. Wallachia (1330) and Moldavia (1359) are born together; Mircea, Vlad and Stephen are one age of brave princes; Trajan's wars and Roman Dacia are one moment; the Romans leaving and the peoples passing through are another; 1913 (Southern Dobruja) folds into independence and the Great Union.
  • Each moment keeps one idea a child can carry away: the Romans came, and Romanian comes from their Latin; three lands, one prince, for a few months; everything joined in 1918.

3. How the words are written

  • Short sentences, one idea each, about 25–45 words a moment: long enough to say what happened, short enough to read aloud before a child turns the page.
  • Names worth meeting again: Burebista, Decebalus, Trajan, Basarab, Bogdan, Stephen the Great, Michael the Brave, Cuza. Children will hear them at school; a story that names them makes them familiar.
  • The time in a child's terms: under each year, "about 1,000 years ago", rounded so it reads like speech (yearsAgo(), from the moment's year).
  • No gore. Vlad is "Vlad III", not by the nickname he is known for: the story is about defending a land, and a seven-year-old needn't meet impaling.
  • Touchy moments told as what happened, without blame. Transylvania in Hungary: "step by step… Romanians, Hungarians and, later, Saxons… all lived there together". 1918: the assemblies "voted to join". 1940: Romania "had to give up lands", to whom, and that northern Transylvania came back. Today: the Republic of Moldova is "a country of its own".
  • Simple, but not wrong (the roadmap's first principle): every sentence is checked below.

4. The facts behind them

MomentChecked
BurebistaKing of the Geto-Dacians c. 82–44 BC; united the tribes into a kingdom from the middle Danube to the Black Sea. "Around 60 BC" falls in his reign.
The RomansTrajan's Dacian wars, 101–102 and 105–106; Decebalus died in 106; the province lasted until Aurelian's withdrawal, c. 271–275: about 165 years. Romanian is a Romance language, from the Latin spoken in the region.
Peoples passing throughGoths, Huns, Gepids, Avars, Slavs, Bulgars and Magyars, 3rd–10th centuries.
Transylvania in HungaryBrought into the Kingdom of Hungary gradually, 10th–13th centuries; Saxon settlers from the Rhine and Moselle lands of the Holy Roman Empire (today's Germany and Luxembourg) arrived in the 12th century.
Basarab and BogdanBasarab I defeated Charles I of Hungary at Posada in November 1330; Bogdan I made Moldavia independent of Hungary in 1359.
Mircea, Vlad, StephenMircea the Elder (1386–1418), Vlad III (ruled 1448, 1456–62, 1476), Stephen the Great (1457–1504), all fighting the Ottomans; Stephen founded dozens of churches and monasteries, Putna and Voroneț among them.
Michael the BravePrince of Wallachia from 1593, Transylvania from 1599, Moldavia from May 1600: the three under one ruler until the autumn of 1600.
Bukovina and BessarabiaBukovina to the Habsburg Monarchy ("Austria") in 1775; Bessarabia to the Russian Empire by the Treaty of Bucharest, 1812.
The UnionAlexandru Ioan Cuza elected prince of Moldavia (January 1859) and Wallachia (February 1859); the name Romania was in official use from 1862 and in the 1866 constitution: "soon called Romania".
IndependenceDeclared 9 May 1877, won in the war of 1877–78, recognised at the Congress of Berlin, 1878, which also gave Romania northern Dobruja and its coast.
The Great UnionBessarabia voted in March/April 1918, Bukovina in November, Transylvania at Alba Iulia on 1 December 1918, Romania's national day since 1990.
1940Bessarabia and northern Bukovina to the Soviet Union (June), northern Transylvania to Hungary (August), Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria (September); northern Transylvania returned to Romania after the war (Paris peace treaty, 1947).
1947Northern Transylvania back in Romania in 1944–45, confirmed by the Paris peace treaty of February 1947; Bessarabia and northern Bukovina stayed with the Soviet Union, Southern Dobruja with Bulgaria. Romania's borders since.
TodayThe revolution of December 1989 ended communist rule; Romania joined the European Union on 1 January 2007; the Republic of Moldova has been independent since 1991.

Checked against the standard textbook account; worth a second read by a teacher before the site goes public (lesson 08's fact check).

5. Icons

Each moment names one icon for the player (lesson 06) to draw: the Dacian wolf-banner, the Roman eagle, a horse for the peoples on the move, a crown, a castle, a shield, a sword, a map for the lands taken, joined hands for the Union, a flag, a storm for 1940 and the EU's star. Only the names for now (MomentIcon), so the drawings can be made in one style together.
